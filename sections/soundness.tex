\section{(Un)Soundness of Method Local Classes}
\label{sec:soundness}
Section \ref{sec:funnelling} already demonstrated the unsoundness of allowing the rule \textsc{new-local-java} in our core language,
echoing Java's unsoundness.
With the full type system developed in Section \ref{sec:core} we can show that funneling definitively resolves the soundness issue:

% With our language primitives in place, we now consider type safety of two variants of our language.
% The two variants differ only in the typing of local classes:
% \begin{enumerate}
% \item $\zeta(\mathsf{Java})$ types local classes with rule \textsc{new-local-java}, as Java does;
% \item $\zeta(\mathsf{funnelled})$ types local classes with rule \textsc{new-local}.
% \end{enumerate}
% Both variants use the same type test:
% rule \textsc{if-instanceof} accepts only checked casts, and at run time rules \textsc{ifThen} and \textsc{ifElse} compare class names only.
% As shown in Section~\ref{sec:java}, $\zeta(\mathsf{Java})$ is unsound.
% We now show that $\zeta(\mathsf{funnelled})$ is sound.

We first show that reduction is deterministic, up to the names chosen by $\mathsf{fresh}$ (which handles $\alpha$-renaming of bindings when expanding method calls).

\begin{theorem}[Determinism]
If $\step{e}{e_1}$ and $\step{e}{e_2}$, then $\mathsf{fresh}(e_1,e_2)$.
\end{theorem}

With this in hand, we may establish type safety via progress and preservation~\cite{wright1994syntactic}.\todo{ref supplementary materials for Lean proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type Safety]
Under a class table $\decls$ such that $\mathsf{Dec}\gap\mathrm{Ok}$ for every $\mathsf{Dec}\in\decls$:
if $\typing{\emptyset}{\emptyset}{e_0}{T}$, and $e_0\rightarrow^* e$, then $\typing{\emptyset}{\emptyset}{e}{T}$ and either $e$ is of form $v$, or there is an $e'$ such that $\step{e}{e'}$.
\end{theorem}

Both theorems are proved in Lean~4 (Appendix~\ref{app:lean}).
The proof of preservation uses the standard lemmas of weakening, substitution and inversion.
% , and these lemmas specific to our core language:
% a renaming by $\mathsf{fresh}$ preserves typing;
% a copy of a local class has the supertype and the method signatures of the declaration of the local class;
% a method has the same signature in every supertype of the class of the receiver;
% a value that passes a type test accepted by the checked cast has the type of the type test;
% a reduction step preserves the well-formedness requirements.
Determinism follows from the unique decomposition of an expression into an evaluation context and a redex.
Appendix~\ref{app:lean:statements} lists all the lemmas.

The formalism of Section~\ref{sec:core} is fully designed by the authors.
The Lean formalization and the two proofs are written by an AI, Claude Opus~5.5, guided by one of the authors.
First, for about 16 hours of computation, the AI wrote Lean definitions as close as possible to the formalism of the paper, without attempting any proof.
A proof attempt gives the AI an incentive to tweak the definitions until the theorems are provable; this first phase has no such incentive.
The output of this first phase is the side by side comparison of Appendix~\ref{app:lean:sidebyside}: 14 pages relating each piece of the formalism of the paper to the corresponding Lean code.
Second, the AI proved the two theorems, in about 2 hours.
Third, for about 8 hours, the AI made the proofs shorter and more readable, and aligned the names inside the Lean code with the names and the terms of the paper; this third phase also used Claude Fable~5.2.
Finally, the AI wrote the 90 validation examples of Appendix~\ref{app:lean:examples}, to check that the Lean definitions behave as the formalism of the paper:
each example states a verdict of the rules of the paper on well-formedness, typing or reduction, and Lean proves that verdict.
An author with significant experience in formalized metatheory, who was not involved in guiding the AI, reviewed the process, side-by-side comparison, and actual Lean artifact, and found no issues.
